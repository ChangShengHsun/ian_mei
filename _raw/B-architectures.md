# B — Architectures & Representations for Fine Curvilinear Structure Segmentation

Scope: model architectures/representations for segmenting/analyzing thin curvilinear
structures in medical images (retinal vessels, coronary/cerebral vasculature, corneal
& peripheral nerves, neurons, airways). Losses, datasets/metrics, and general CV are
out of scope (covered by other agents). Weighted toward 2023–2026 work.

Verification protocol: `[verified: URL]` means a page was actually loaded (WebFetch)
confirming title + venue + year. `[unverified]` means only a search snippet was seen,
or the page could not be loaded (paywall/403/binary PDF) even though the paper likely
exists. No citation below was invented; ambiguous/unloadable ones are explicitly
flagged.

## Which family is actually SOTA in 2026, and how confident

**No single family "wins" outright; the field is fragmented by what "winning" means.**

- On the classic pixel-metric leaderboards (DRIVE/CHASE_DB1/STARE for retinal vessels), the actual leader keeps rotating among incremental CNN-attention variants (FR-UNet-class, CS2-Net-class) and now Mamba variants (Serp-Mamba, HM-Mamba) — these papers report new SOTA against the *previous* paper's numbers, but I found no independent, apples-to-apples 2026 leaderboard re-ranking all of them at once. Confidence: **low** that any one architecture is durably "the" SOTA; **medium-high** confidence that pure-CNN U-Net variants (plain nnU-Net) are no longer the ceiling — multiple 2024–2025 papers (U-Mamba, DSCNet, Serp-Mamba) report beating strong CNN/nnU-Net-class baselines on their respective tasks.
- On **topological correctness** (not just pixel overlap) — arguably the metric that matters most for vessel/nerve trees since a broken 1-pixel gap is clinically meaningful — graph-first/centerline-first methods (GraphMorph, Relationformer-style graph generation, DSCNet's topology loss) show the most consistent, mechanism-grounded gains, because they optimize connectivity directly rather than as a side effect of pixel accuracy. Confidence: **medium-high** this is the right direction, based on GraphMorph (NeurIPS 2024) and DSCNet (ICCV 2023) both reporting topology-metric gains specifically, not just Dice.
- On **foundation models**, the evidence is unusually clear and negative: generic SAM/MedSAM demonstrably fail on thin branching structures, and the WACV 2026 "Quantifying the Limits" paper argues this is architectural (fine-tuning doesn't fix it), not a data-scale problem. Vessel-specific foundation models (vesselFM) and LoRA-adapted SAM (VesselSAM) close much of the gap but are new (2024–2025) and evaluated on narrower benchmarks than the general SAM literature. Confidence: **high** that off-the-shelf SAM/MedSAM is NOT the answer for vessels; **medium** that vessel-specific foundation models have actually closed the gap broadly (evidence is still early and modality-limited).
- **Overall honest read**: no architecture family has "solved" fine curvilinear segmentation. The strongest 2024–2026 signal is that (a) topology/graph-aware objectives and representations outperform pure pixel-wise ones on the metric that actually matters, and (b) generic foundation models are a genuine, mechanistically-explained dead end for this specific problem class, which is a meaningful negative result to build a thesis on.

## Hype check — popular-looking but weak/unreplicated evidence

- **SAM / SAM2 / MedSAM "zero-shot medical segmentation"** — widely cited and demoed, but for vessels specifically the evidence is that it fails, and the WACV 2026 diagnostic paper shows fine-tuning doesn't fix it either. The hype (SAM as a universal segmenter) and the vessel-specific reality (consistently poor without heavy architecture surgery) are in direct tension — worth stating explicitly rather than assuming SAM adapters are a shortcut.
- **Wavelet/frequency-branch papers (WaveRNet, FreqUNet, WS-SAM, WDM-UNet)** — a cluster of very similar-sounding 2024–2026 papers (dual-branch wavelet decomposition + spatial fusion) each claim "SOTA," but they mostly compare against each other and older CNN baselines within narrow retinal-fundus benchmarks; I found no independent replication or cross-paper benchmark table. Treat "frequency-domain branch improves vessel continuity" as plausible but not yet consolidated evidence.
- **Frangi-Net / learnable-Hessian-filter approaches** — conceptually appealing (bring classical vesselness into a differentiable pipeline) but I could not locate a dedicated, well-cited benchmark paper with numeric head-to-head comparisons; the concept surfaces mainly in passing/citation form in other papers. Likely under-evidenced relative to how often the idea is referenced.
- **RL/agent-based centerline tracing (DQN-style tracing agents)** — intuitive and reports good numbers on internal benchmarks (Zhang & Wang-type MICCAI 2018 work, later PPO variants), but adoption and follow-up replication in the broader 2023–2026 literature looks thin compared to the graph-generation line (Relationformer/GraphMorph), which seems to have superseded step-by-step agent tracing as the more actively developed approach.
- **Diffusion-based segmentation for curvilinear structures** — very early (CurvSegFlow is a June 2026 preprint, not yet peer-reviewed at time of this scan; C-DARL 2023 result not independently re-verified here). The framing "diffusion models are the next step for vessel segmentation" is more a narrative extrapolated from diffusion's general 2023–2024 momentum in medical imaging (MedSegDiff-V2 etc., not vessel-specific) than evidence specific to thin curvilinear structures.
