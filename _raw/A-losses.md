| **Skeleton Recall Loss** | Kirchhoff, Rokuss, Roy, Kovacs, Ulrich, Wald, Zenk, Vollmuth, Kleesiek, Isensee, Maier-Hein, "Skeleton Recall Loss for Connectivity Conserving and Resource Efficient Segmentation of Thin Tubular Structures", ECCV 2024 (arXiv:2404.03010) | Same connectivity-preservation goal as clDice/TopoLoss, but their GPU soft-skeletonization is too expensive/memory-heavy for large 3D volumes and multi-class problems | Skeletonizes only the GT (cheap, CPU, precomputed offline) instead of both pred and GT on GPU each iteration; trains a soft recall loss of the prediction against the fixed GT skeleton; first topology loss claimed to be multi-class capable | Skeletonization asymmetry (GT-only) means it cannot directly penalize spurious extra structure the network hallucinates the same way clDice's two-sided formulation can; recall-only bias needs to be balanced with a precision-side loss (e.g. Dice/CE) in practice | [verified: https://arxiv.org/abs/2404.03010, https://github.com/MIC-DKFZ/Skeleton-Recall, https://www.ecva.net/papers/eccv_2024/papers_ECCV/papers/09904.pdf] |
| **Topograph** | Lux, Berger, Weers, Stucki, Rueckert, Bauer, Paetzold, "Topograph: An efficient Graph-Based Framework for Strictly Topology Preserving Image Segmentation", ICLR 2025 (arXiv:2411.03228) | Prior topology losses "lack robust topological guarantees, are limited to specific use cases, or impose high computational costs" | Builds a component graph encoding topology of pred+GT jointly; identifies topologically-critical *regions* (not just pixels) via graph structure; claims a strict, formally guaranteed homotopy-equivalence metric, ~5x faster loss computation than persistent-homology alternatives | Still needs a discrete graph-construction step per iteration (connected-component labeling at scale); guarantees are about *this* graph abstraction, not proven equivalent to true continuous homotopy in all edge cases | [verified: https://arxiv.org/abs/2411.03228; ICLR 2025 confirmed via OpenReview https://openreview.net/forum?id=Q0zmmNNePz] |

## How these losses relate / lineage

Two independent families converged on the same problem (thin-structure connectivity) from different math:

1. **Persistent-homology family** (topology-first, count-then-location): TopoLoss (Hu 2019, NeurIPS) →
   Clough et al. (TPAMI 2020/22, Betti-number priors without GT) → **Betti Matching** (Stucki 2022/23,
   ICML — fixes "right count, wrong place" by spatially matching barcode features) → **Efficient Betti
   Matching** (2024, pure speed engineering) → **Spatial-Aware Persistent Feature Matching** (2024,
   folds image-space info into the matching step) → **PI-Att** (2024, alternate persistence-image
   encoding) → **TopoSculpt** (2025, whole-volume + curriculum). This family is mathematically the most
   rigorous (true homotopy/Betti-number guarantees) but historically the most expensive; almost every
   later paper in this line is explicitly a speed or spatial-accuracy fix on the one before it.

2. **Skeleton/clDice family** (geometry-first, cheap differentiable proxy): **clDice** (Shit 2021,
   CVPR — soft-skeletonization as conv ops, no real persistent homology) → **Skeleton Recall Loss**
   (2024, ECCV — makes it cheaper by skeletonizing only GT, offline) → **cbDice** (2024, MICCAI — adds
   vessel-radius weighting to fix clDice's diameter-imbalance) → **Centerline-Cross-Entropy** (2024,
   MICCAI — swaps clDice's Dice base for CE for robustness) → **Smooth clDice** (2025 — smooths
   noisy-annotation sensitivity) → **ContextLoss** (2025, ICIP — widens the critical-pixel mask to a
   neighborhood). **Homotopy Warping** (Hu 2022, NeurIPS) and **Topograph** (2025, ICLR) sit at the
   junction of both families: they use lightweight digital-topology/graph structures (cheaper than full
   persistent homology) to find "critical pixels/regions" the way clDice's skeleton does, but aim for
   Betti-family-style topological guarantees.

3. **Boundary/distance family** (geometry of the interface, no topology machinery at all): **Active
   Contour loss** (Chen 2019, CVPR, classical snake-inspired length+region terms) and **Boundary loss**
   (Kervadec 2019, MIDL/MedIA) both react to the same problem — Dice/CE's region-integral imbalance on
   small/thin targets — from opposite directions (AC adds a boundary-length regularizer *on top of*
   region terms; Boundary loss *replaces* the region integral with a contour-distance integral to avoid
   the imbalance altogether). **Hausdorff-distance loss** (Karimi 2019, TMI) and the **DT-map empirical
   study** (Ma 2020, MIDL) sit downstream of both, using the same distance-transform machinery to
   target the worst-case boundary metric directly. **BoundaryDoU** (2023, MICCAI) and **RMI** (2019,
   NeurIPS) are the region-only outliers in this family — they get boundary/neighborhood sensitivity
   without a distance map, at the cost of no explicit geometric distance bound.

4. **Convergence point:** by 2023–2025, nearly every new paper (cbDice, clCE, ContextLoss, Topograph,
   Spatial-Aware Matching) is explicitly framed as "fixes weakness W of clDice/Betti-Matching" rather
   than a from-scratch idea — the sub-field has visibly entered an incremental-refinement phase around
   these two 2019–2021 anchors (TopoLoss and clDice), not a phase of new fundamental formulations.

## Still open (concretely-stated unsolved problems)

1. **No loss simultaneously guarantees topology AND diameter/width correctness at low cost.**
   clDice-family losses are cheap but diameter-blind (fixed only partially by cbDice's radius
   weighting, which itself depends on a possibly-noisy skeleton-radius estimate); persistent-homology
   losses give real topological guarantees but ignore width entirely and are expensive. The single
   Jan-2026 preprint attempting to fold width into a topology constraint (arXiv:2601.11409) is
   unreviewed and adds a PDE-smoothing solver, so "cheap + width-aware + topologically guaranteed" has
   no accepted, efficient solution yet — this is unsolved because every attempt so far has had to trade
   away one of the three properties (cost, width-awareness, formal topology guarantee).

2. **All topology losses assume the ground-truth skeleton/topology itself is correct — none handle
   annotator disagreement or partial-volume ambiguity on sub-pixel-width structures.** Smooth clDice's
   "uncertainty zone" is the only located attempt in this scan, and it is an unreviewed technical
   report, not a peer-reviewed fix. Vessels/nerves at the resolution limit of the imaging modality (≈1
   voxel wide) have GT masks that are themselves inconsistent between annotators, so a loss that trusts
   the GT skeleton as ground truth is optimizing against noise near the true image-resolution limit —
   unsolved because it requires either a probabilistic/uncertainty-aware skeleton definition or
   annotator-consensus datasets that mostly don't exist yet for fine vascular/neural structures.

3. **No loss directly targets *why* a break happens (a knowledge/capability failure of the network)
   vs. merely penalizing *that* it happened.** Every loss surveyed here — from TopoLoss through
   TopoSculpt — is a training-time penalty on the geometric/topological *symptom* (a gap, a missing
   loop, a Betti-number mismatch). None of them diagnose whether the break is caused by a local
   contrast/SNR failure the network genuinely cannot resolve (a knowledge limit) vs. an
   architecture/training capacity limit — this framing (symptom-level loss vs. cause-level diagnosis)
   is exactly the sub-area gap adjacent to what the parent survey (VLA-Doctor-style failure-mode
   analysis) is asking about, and it is unsolved because it requires attaching a causal/diagnostic
   probe to the loss's failure cases, which none of the surveyed papers attempt.

4. **3D, multi-class, and compute-cost are still in tension.** Skeleton Recall Loss (2024) claims the
   first "multi-class capable" thin-structure loss and a 90% compute reduction, but this is only ~1.5
   years old and not yet cross-validated by independent groups outside the original nnU-Net-adjacent
   ecosystem; Betti-Matching-family losses (the mathematically strongest guarantee) are still described
   in their own 2024 follow-up as needing a custom C++ implementation to be merely *usable* at 3D scale,
   not fast. A loss that is simultaneously (a) full 3D volume-scale, (b) multi-class, (c) has a proven
   topological guarantee, and (d) trains in comparable wall-clock time to plain Dice/CE does not yet
   exist in the papers found in this scan — each existing method sacrifices at least one of these four.
