| Drop an Octave (Octave Convolution) — Chen et al. | ICCV 2019 | Splits feature maps into high- and low-frequency groups processed at different spatial resolutions, explicitly separating "fine edge/texture" from "coarse shape" computation. | Gives an explicit computational budget knob for "how much capacity goes to fine detail vs. global layout" — useful if compute must be spent disproportionately on thin/faint structures rather than uniformly. | Medium — needs re-tuning of the high/low frequency ratio for medical-scale images; not validated on any medical task as far as this scan found. | frequency architecture |

## Techniques from vision that have NOT yet been tried on medical curvilinear structures, as far as this scan could verify

Each item below: what's untried + an honest confidence statement about the "untried" claim itself (absence of evidence is weak evidence of absence — these are searched, not exhaustively proven).

1. **Anti-aliased downsampling (BlurPool, Zhang 2019) as a stated design choice in a vessel/nerve segmentation backbone.**
   Confidence: **medium**. No paper was found explicitly crediting BlurPool for a thin-structure medical gain, but BlurPool is now bundled in some standard model libraries, so it may be silently present (and un-highlighted) in some medical pipelines without being called out as the reason for a result. Absence of an explicit paper is not strong evidence of absence of use.

2. **Octave Convolution's explicit high/low-frequency compute split, applied to vascular/neural texture.**
   Confidence: **medium-high** (fairly confident it's genuinely untried). Searches for "octave convolution" + medical/vessel/curvilinear returned nothing; the technique is also somewhat out of fashion (2019, no follow-ups found in the 2023-2026 window), so it may simply be an abandoned direction rather than an unexplored one — worth checking why it didn't catch on before assuming it's a free opportunity.

3. **FeatUp/LoftUp-style frozen-foundation-model feature upsampling as the front end of a medical thin-structure segmenter.**
   Confidence: **medium-high**. No result in this scan combined "FeatUp" or "LoftUp" with any medical/vessel/curvilinear keyword. Given both are 2024/2025 general-purpose tools, a medical application would very plausibly not exist yet purely on recency grounds — this is a genuinely open, low-hanging opportunity rather than a hidden prior result.

4. **The Geirhos texture/shape-bias cue-conflict diagnostic, run on a trained medical fine-texture model.**
   Confidence: **medium**. This scan did not search medical-imaging venues directly (out of lane — that's a different sub-agent's job), so a medical instantiation of this exact diagnostic may already exist there; within general-CV sources, no cross-over was found.

5. **Hallucination Score-style (or any MLLM-based) hallucination auditing applied specifically to super-resolved/enhanced diagnostic images before clinical use.**
   Confidence: **medium**. General medical-imaging hallucination concerns are well-documented qualitatively (see warnings below), but a quantitative hallucination-detection metric transplanted from the Ren et al. 2025 paper specifically was not found in a medical instantiation.

6. **SEMIR-style graph-minor / supernode collapse for full-resolution inference without tiling, applied to whole-slide or volumetric neuro-vascular images.**
   Confidence: **low-medium** (the paper itself is only ~6 weeks old at scan time and unreviewed) — genuinely too new for anyone to have applied it anywhere yet, medical or not.

## Warning list: vision tricks that would be actively dangerous in a diagnostic setting

- **Diffusion/GAN-based super-resolution or texture enhancement applied to a diagnostic image before a human or model makes a call.** The perception-distortion trade-off is a proven mathematical result (you cannot maximize both realism and fidelity simultaneously) [verified: arXiv:2507.14367 discussion] — any "sharper-looking" vessel/nerve texture produced this way may be a plausible fabrication, not a true recovered structure. This is the single highest-severity item on this list.
- **Any perceptual loss (VGG/LPIPS-style) used to train a restoration or enhancement network on medical images.** Perceptual losses explicitly optimize for "looks like textures the network has seen," which is precisely the mechanism that produces confident-looking but false fine structure.
- **Trusting SAM/SAM2/DINOv2 zero-shot or frozen-feature outputs on sub-patch-width structures without checking patch size against expected vessel/nerve width.** These models' patch tokenization (typically 14-16px) can silently and total-drop structures thinner than a patch — with no visible failure signal, the model just doesn't represent that information at all, distinct from a "low confidence" failure a clinician might notice.
- **Stylized/texture-randomization augmentation (from the Geirhos shape-bias line) applied naively as a generic "robustness" augmentation to medical training data.** In natural images this trades texture reliance for shape reliance; in a domain where the pathology IS the texture (fine neuro-vascular texture), the same augmentation could train the model to systematically discount the exact signal being studied. Only safe as a deliberate, controlled ablation — not as a default augmentation recipe.
- **Bilinear/second-order pooling or other global-pooling texture descriptors used anywhere a precise spatial location of a thin structure matters.** These descriptors are designed to be translation-invariant summaries — exactly the property that destroys localization information needed for a diagnostic finding tied to a specific location.
